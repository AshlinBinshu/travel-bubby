## Key question 1: Which newspaper(s), which sections, and what do they cost in 2026?

### Takeaway
The consensus across coaching institutes, IIM alumni on InsideIIM and interview transcripts is "one general daily + one business daily, read for editorials/economy rather than headlines": The Hindu or Indian Express for the general paper and Mint, Economic Times or Business Standard for business. In 2026 all of these are metered-paywall products costing roughly ₹1,000–2,600 a year for digital access (Mint Premium ~₹1,499, Business Standard Smart Annual ₹1,999, The Hindu digital ~₹999–1,499 on sale, Indian Express ~₹1,199–1,699, ET Prime ~₹2,549), with The Hindu documented at 10 free articles/month and Indian Express/Mint metered at an unpublished number.

## Key question 2: Business/economy newsletters and explainers — which are free and short?

### Takeaway
Finshots (free, one email a morning, self-described 3-minute read) is the only widely-cited daily explainer that is both free and short; PRS Legislative Research's Monthly Policy Review and PIB's Economic Survey/Budget summaries are free, official-grade monthly/annual reads. The Ken (~₹5,999/yr app price; $75–125/yr on its site) and The Morning Context ($99/yr list, $50 intro, ~₹3,000 app price, 40% student discount) are paid long-form products.

### Inferences
- Inshorts is best treated as a 2-minute headline scan to decide which one or two stories to read in full in a newspaper; the depth-probing nature of IIM interviews (numbers, causes, consequences, your stance) is exactly what a 60-word summary omits.
- Because the documented practice is a physical diary/notebook, a Notion database with the same fields (topic, date, key facts, stakeholders, for/against, India link, your view, likely follow-up) is a faithful digital equivalent, but this is a design inference, not something any source prescribes.
