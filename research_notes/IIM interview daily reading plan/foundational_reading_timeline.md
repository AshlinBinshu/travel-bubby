## Key Question 1: CAT 2026 / IIM 2027 admissions timeline (exam, result, shortlists, WAT/PI windows, CAP)

### Takeaway
CAT 2026 is on Sunday 29 November 2026 (IIM Indore convening), with the result tentatively in the third week of December 2026 (the 2025 cycle's result came on 24 Dec 2025, not early January). Using the 2026 cycle as a proxy, expect IIM A/C shortlists ~8 Jan, IIM B ~13 Jan, IIM K ~16 Jan 2027, interviews concentrated in February–March 2027, CAP (now only three IIMs) interviews late Feb–end March, and final results April–May 2027.

- ESTIMATED 2027 calendar for planning (derived by shifting 2026-cycle dates forward): CAT result ~ 20–31 Dec 2026; IIM A/C shortlists ~ 7–10 Jan 2027; IIM B ~ 12–15 Jan; IIM K ~ 15–18 Jan; IIM L/I and others mid-to-late January; CAP EOI last two weeks of January; interviews 1 Feb – end March 2027 (CAP to ~31 Mar; some IIMs into early April); final results late April – May 2027.
- The practical "reading window" between CAT and the first interview is therefore ~9–10 weeks (29 Nov → early Feb), of which only ~3–4 weeks fall after the result; the user's December block is the only unpressured foundational-reading slot.
- Because the 2025 result came on 24 Dec, the plan should assume the result can arrive before Christmas rather than in January.

## Key Question 2: Books most recommended for IIM PI/WAT, and which are actually asked about

### Takeaway
No 2024–26 IIM-convert source surfaced that prescribes a canonical list of economy/business/biography titles (Ramesh Singh, Rajan & Lamba, Lean Startup, etc.); the evidence instead shows (a) three interview-prep handbooks repeatedly named by PaGaLGuY, (b) an IIM-A alumnus insisting on reading for genuine interest, and (c) multiple interview transcripts where panels probe whatever book the candidate names (favourite line, plot summary, author's other works, link to Indian policy). The "right" book is one the candidate can defend in depth.

### Cited Findings

**Interview-prep handbooks (dated 2019 article)**
- "Conquering CAT (MBA) Interview" by N.S. Uppal: "Most aspirants found this book very useful while preparing for their CAT MBA interviews" — [PaGaLGuY books and magazines to read](https://www.pagalguy.com/articles/best-preparation-tips-for-cat-interview-find-out-books-and-magazines-to-read?amp=1)
- "How to Get Into Your Dream B-School" by Chayan Mukhopadhyay: "can help you prepare for the interview round and GD/WAT" — same [PaGaLGuY article](https://www.pagalguy.com/articles/best-preparation-tips-for-cat-interview-find-out-books-and-magazines-to-read?amp=1)
- "I'm Not Afraid of GDPI" by Tuhina Varshney "thoroughly discusses the GD-PI stages" — same [PaGaLGuY article](https://www.pagalguy.com/articles/best-preparation-tips-for-cat-interview-find-out-books-and-magazines-to-read?amp=1)

**How to choose general reading (alumni advice)**
- IIM Ahmedabad alumnus: "If you read to impress the panel, you'll be more concerned with completing the number of books than with gaining a deeper understanding"; history, politics, innovation and literature are good conversation genres; "read about the author and his/her other works as well" — [Careers360 Q&A: books to be mastered in IIM interview](https://www.careers360.com/question-can-anyone-pls-suggest-some-books-to-be-mastered-in-iim-interview)
- IIM alumni recommendations include "Flowers for Algernon" and "The Complete Novels of Sherlock Holmes" (framed as personal-growth reading, 2019) — [InsideIIM 15 must-read books](https://insideiim.com/10-books-mba-recommendations-iim-alumni)
- An IIM Calcutta 2020-22 admit read "Animal Farm", "Sapiens" and Amish's Shiva trilogy, originally to improve RC — [InsideIIM: My journey to IIM Calcutta](https://insideiim.com/my-journey-to-iim-calcutta-and-what-i-learned-on-the-way-feat-akash-iim-calcutta-mba-2020-22)

**Evidence of how panels actually test "books read"**
- IIM Calcutta 2020 WAT-PI: "They asked me about my favourite books and the famous line from one of my favourite books" — [InsideIIM IIM-C WAT-PI 2020 experience](https://insideiim.com/iim-calcutta-wat-pi-2020-experience-amrita-mishra)
- IIM Lucknow: candidate listing reading/blogging as hobbies was asked favourite author, favourite genre, Booker Prize winners from India, then the interviewer read the candidate's blog — [InsideIIM IIM-L interview experience](https://insideiim.com/iim-lucknow-interview-experience-pratik-ranjan)
- Panel asked a candidate to summarise the plot of Dan Brown's "Inferno" after she named it as a favourite, then to connect its theme to a real Indian policy — [The Better India IIM interview questions](https://thebetterindia.com/267106/iim-interview-questions-preparation-tips-iim-experiences-cat-quora)
- Careers360 hobby-question guide: a candidate who "read mostly fiction" was asked for favourites; "The candidate was fully aware about his hobby ... and was able to answer the questions convincingly" — [Careers360 hobbies questions](https://bschool.careers360.com/articles/iim-personal-interview-how-tackle-questions-related-your-hobbies)
- IIM Nagpur: "After spending some 10-12 minutes discussing my work experience, panellists browsed through my application form and quizzed about my interests and hobbies" — [InsideIIM IIM Nagpur experience](https://insideiim.com/iim-nagpur-interview-experience-sundeep-undi-iim-nagpur/)

**Indian-economy primers (evidence is UPSC-oriented, not IIM-specific)**
- Ramesh Singh "Indian Economy" (McGraw Hill, 18th ed.) is marketed for "Prelims, Mains, and Interview preparation" (civil services); chapters run from introduction to economics through national income, planning, external sector and public finance — [McGraw Hill India courseware listing](https://india.magento.intl.prod.mheducation.com/courseware-on-indian-economy-9789364446570-india); [Rau's IAS how to prepare Indian economy](https://compass.rauias.com/blog/prepare-indian-economy/)
- Sanjeev Verma "The Indian Economy" (Unique, 14e) is listed as the "beginner-friendly ... simpler language, shorter chapters" alternative — [EaseMyPrep UPSC economy strategy 2026](https://easemyprep.in/blog/upsc-economy-preparation-strategy-2026-complete-guide)
- NCERT Class 11 "Indian Economic Development" (pre-1991 history, planning, employment, infrastructure) and Class 12 Macroeconomics (GDP, money, banking, government budget) are the recommended foundation — [EaseMyPrep](https://easemyprep.in/blog/upsc-economy-preparation-strategy-2026-complete-guide); [Rau's IAS](https://compass.rauias.com/blog/prepare-indian-economy/)
- "Breaking the Mould" (Raghuram Rajan & Rohit Lamba, 2023) "analyses challenges facing the Indian economy, critiques past and present economic policies, and proposes strategies for inclusive growth", "shorn of the technical jargon found in most textbooks", with "an unmistakeable dose of politics" — [Wikipedia: Breaking the Mould](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking_the_Mould); [Business Standard review](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/book/india-s-growth-story-a-question-of-answers-123122801089_1.html); [Commonwealth Round Table review](https://www.commonwealthroundtable.co.uk/general/books/book-review-breaking-the-mold/)

### Inferences
- For a 30-min/day budget, the evidence supports at most one "defendable" general book (any genre the candidate genuinely likes) plus one economy primer that is skimmed rather than read cover-to-cover (NCERT Class 12 Macro chapters or the Ramesh Singh/Sanjeev Verma chapters on national income, inflation, monetary/fiscal policy, external sector). "Breaking the Mould" is the most interview-suitable of the economy titles because it is short, argumentative and current, and invites the "what do you think of Rajan's view?" follow-up.
- The three GD-PI handbooks are frameworks, not reading per se; one of them in December is sufficient.
- Business classics (Lean Startup, Zero to One, Good to Great, Hard Thing About Hard Things), biographies (Tata, Ambani, Murthy, Musk, Jobs) and "Sapiens/Thinking Fast and Slow/Freakonomics" were not evidenced in any surfaced IIM-interview source as being asked about or recommended by converts; treat them as optional hobby reading, valuable only if the candidate will list them and can discuss them.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 Reddit r/CATpreparation, Quora or InsideIIM thread with a converts' book list was retrievable (search returned only 2017–2021 content, and Reddit did not appear in results). Specific claims that "The Tatas", "The Polyester Prince", "India's Long Road", "The Price of Time" or Sanjeev Sanyal's books are commonly recommended for IIM PI could not be sourced and should not be asserted.
- No source quantified how often "last book you read" is asked across interviews.

## Key Question 3: Reports and documents to skim (Economic Survey, Budget, RBI, indices) with 2025/2026 values

### Takeaway
The four documents that interview coaching repeatedly points to are the Economic Survey 2025-26 (29 Jan 2026), Union Budget 2026-27 (1 Feb 2026), the RBI's latest policy (7 Oct 2026: repo raised to 5.50%, first hike since Feb 2023) and the headline indices; the key numbers a candidate should carry are listed below with sources.

### Cited Findings

## Key Question 4: "Static" knowledge areas panels test that daily news does not cover

### Takeaway
Interview transcripts show panels spend 10–12 minutes on work experience, then probe graduation fundamentals at definition level (a B.Com asked to define current ratio, net worth, return on sales), home-state facts (tourist spots, a region's water problem), and the candidate's ability to explain their job to a layperson; these are preparable in advance and distinct from news.

### Cited Findings
- Three recurring question families per a March-2026 guide: "common personal questions, academic questions, and questions related to work experience and hobbies" — [Careers360 IIM interview questions 2026-28](https://www.careers360.com/mba/articles/iim-interview-questions-2026-28-mba-admission-pi-questions-btech-commers-economics-grads-gk-topics)
- Academic depth example (IIM Nagpur, B.Com): panel asked the candidate "to name the subjects studied in graduation, define current ratio, explain net worth, and explain return on sales" — [Hitbullseye IIM Nagpur GDPI experiences](https://gdpiportal.hitbullseye.com/iim-nagpur-gdpi-experiences.php)
- Economics graduate: "A panelist noted the candidate had done a graduation in economics and asked what the favourite subject was" (answer: managerial economics) — [Hitbullseye IIM Nagpur](https://gdpiportal.hitbullseye.com/iim-nagpur-gdpi-experiences.php)

## Key Question 5: Phasing from October to the interview (what mentors and converts suggest)

### Takeaway
Mentors converge on: reflect briefly right after CAT, then start WAT-PI groundwork in December without waiting for the result ("only 4–6 weeks remain before interviews begin"), build a daily schedule and a current-events diary with background notes, switch to institute-specific prep on receiving calls in January, and run 2–3 mock interviews before February.

### Cited Findings
- Post-CAT: spend "a few hours reflecting on the attempt while the experience is fresh"; avoid obsessing over answer keys and percentile predictors; "constant comparison with peers only creates stress" — [Jaipuria post-CAT reflection](https://www.jaipuria.ac.in/blog/post-cat-reflection-analyzing-attempts-and-preparing-for-the-next-step/)
- Start WAT-PI prep early: waiting for results is a common mistake since "only 4–6 weeks remain before interviews begin by then"; "the first step to preparing for the WAT-PI round is to prepare a schedule" — [Jaipuria preparing for WAT-PI](https://www.jaipuria.ac.in/blog/preparing-for-wat-pi-mba-admission-journey/)
- PI groundwork: "Revise your graduation subjects, work projects, and internships"; be ready for hobbies, current affairs, work experience; mock interviews with peers/mentors — [Jaipuria preparing for WAT-PI](https://www.jaipuria.ac.in/blog/preparing-for-wat-pi-mba-admission-journey/)
- Diary method: "making a specific diary and noting all the current events happening nationally and internationally. Every time you come across news which you think is relevant and can be discussed in the interviews, you should keep a note of it" — [InsideIIM IIM-L pre-interview preparation](https://insideiim.com/iim-lucknow-decoding-the-iim-selection-process-pre-interview-preparation-1) (attribution from search extract)
- Go beyond headlines: "jotting down the important events in a notebook and trying to learn about its background or the underlying causes" — [Jaro Education how to prepare for IIT and IIM](https://www.jaroeducation.com/blog/how-to-prepare-for-iit-and-iim)
- News window: the 2020-cycle IIM guide said "an idea about things that happened since the start of 2019 should suffice" (i.e., ~12–14 months) — [InsideIIM IIM-L pre-interview preparation](https://insideiim.com/iim-lucknow-decoding-the-iim-selection-process-pre-interview-preparation-1); a UPSC-oriented guide recommends "at least the 6 months preceding your interview date, with the most intensive focus on the 3 months immediately before" and a four-part note per event (facts, why it matters, own position, strongest counter-argument) — [Bharat Notes current affairs for interview](https://bharatnotes.com/faq/upsc-interview-preparation/current-affairs-preparation-interview/)
- Sources for the diary: The Hindu ("clutter-free, clear and crisp news"), The Economic Times, The Economist — [Jaro Education](https://www.jaroeducation.com/blog/how-to-prepare-for-iit-and-iim); [Careers360 GD-PI Q&A](https://www.careers360.com/question-how-a-student-prepare-for-gd-pi-and-what-plse-advise)
- Mocks: "at least 2-3 mock interviews to an experienced and senior panel of people who can assess your preparation and provide feedback" — [Careers360 GD-PI Q&A](https://www.careers360.com/question-how-a-student-prepare-for-gd-pi-and-what-plse-advise)
- On receiving calls (January): check scores/shortlists immediately, then move to institute-specific format prep — [InsideIIM MBA admissions after CAT results](https://insideiim.com/amp/mba-admissions-2022-after-cat-results)
- Focus on "understanding the context and implications of events rather than just memorizing facts" — [Careers360 IIM interview questions 2026-28](https://www.careers360.com/mba/articles/iim-interview-questions-2026-28-mba-admission-pi-questions-btech-commers-economics-grads-gk-topics)

### Inferences
- Phased 30-min/day plan consistent with the evidence and the Q1 calendar: Oct–28 Nov 2026: CAT-dominant; reading limited to the news diary habit (5 min) and one hobby book. 30 Nov–24 Dec: economy primer chapters (NCERT macro / Ramesh Singh core chapters), one GD-PI handbook, write the home-state and graduation "topic sheets". 24 Dec–mid Jan (result + calls): Economic Survey 2025-26 (PRS summary), Budget 2026-27 highlights, RBI Oct/Dec statements; start 2–3 WATs/week. Mid-Jan–Mar: institute-specific formats, 2–3 mocks, refresh numbers sheet after Economic Survey 2026-27 (~late Jan 2027) and Budget 2027-28 (1 Feb 2027), and keep the diary current with the last 3 months weighted most.
- A "last 30 days" compilation is best built from the candidate's own diary rather than bought, because the evidence stresses background/causes and personal positions over headline recall.

### Gaps
- No 2024–26 IIM-convert account giving an explicit month-by-month (Dec/Jan/Feb) reading split was retrieved before the search budget was exhausted; the phasing above is inferred from coaching/B-school guidance plus the calendar.
- No source quantified a recommended news look-back window specifically for IIMs in 2025-26 (the 12-month vs 6-month figures come from a 2020 IIM guide and a UPSC guide respectively).

## Key Question 6: WAT/AWT writing practice — frequency, format, topic sources

### Takeaway
Guides suggest one timed essay every 2–3 days (≈2–3 per week) at 250–500 words, with the IIM Lucknow format documented as 15 minutes/300 words and other IIMs ranging 10–30 minutes and 200–400 words; topics are mostly current-affairs and social-concern themes, so a bank of 30–40 topics should be built from the news diary.

### Cited Findings
- "Write one short essay every 2–3 days on varied topics"; essays need introduction, body, conclusion; sample topics: "India's growth story depends on its digital economy", "Can Artificial Intelligence replace human intelligence?", "Failure is a stepping stone to success", "Social media has done more harm than good" — [Jaipuria cracking the WAT](https://www.jaipuria.ac.in/blog/cracking-the-written-ability-test-wat/)
- "Practice about 3-4 essays each with varying word limits, for instance, 250, 300, 400, 500 words. The more you practice 300 word articles, better your final 300 word actual WAT is going to be" — [2IIM 3 things to ace WAT](https://online.2iim.com/cat-exam/blogs/cat-preparation-strategy/3-things-to-ace-wat-for-iim-interviews/)
- IIM Lucknow WAT: "Duration: 15 minutes, Word Limit: 300 words" (2019-20 page); topics included "Instant justice is justified", "Education sans employability will result into an inefficient economy", "Priority of Political Parties: National security v/s social security?" — [Hitbullseye IIM Lucknow WAT](https://gdpiportal.hitbullseye.com/iim-lucknow-wat.php)
- "The time given for the WAT round varies from 10-30 minutes with 5-10 minutes to collect and organize ideas. The word limit could be around 200-400 words"; select "the top 3-4 ideas from rough notes before writing" — [PaGaLGuY how to prepare for WAT](https://www.pagalguy.com/articles/how-to-prepare-for-the-written-ability-test-wat-conducted-by-iims-4618?amp=1); [PaGaLGuY how to prepare for essay writing](https://www.pagalguy.com/articles/how-to-prepare-for-essay-writing-5240)
- "The most common topics that appear in written ability tests encompass the domain of current affairs ... either narrative or argumentative" — [Unstop WAT concepts](https://unstop.com/blog/building-your-concepts-about-the-written-ability-test/amp)
- Theme areas: Indian polity, economy, foreign policy, business and commerce, technology, social concerns; "A very important emerging theme ... has been the focus on Social concern" — [MBAUniverse WAT preparation](https://www.mbauniverse.com/wat-for-iims/wat-preparation)
- "WAT tests not only the candidate's knowledge on the topic but also the writing ability, clarity of thought, and structure in writing"; read newspapers and business magazines daily and practise short timed essays — [Jaipuria preparing for WAT-PI](https://www.jaipuria.ac.in/blog/preparing-for-wat-pi-mba-admission-journey/); [InsideIIM the WAT-PI round](https://insideiim.com/the-wat-pi-round-for-iim-admissions)
- Build a bank of "30–40 common WAT topics and practice systematically" — [Jaipuria cracking the WAT](https://www.jaipuria.ac.in/blog/cracking-the-written-ability-test-wat/)

### Inferences
- Within 30 min/day, a 15-min timed 300-word WAT plus 10 min self-review fits on alternate days from late December; this yields ~25–30 practised essays before February, matching the 30–40-topic bank guidance.
- Past WAT topic lists on Hitbullseye/InsideIIM plus the candidate's own diary headlines are sufficient topic sources; no paid topic bank is needed.

### Gaps
- 2025/2026 WAT formats (time/word limits) for IIM A (AWT), B, C, K, I were not verified; the only format evidence is IIM-L 2019-20.
- No source gave an evidence-based optimum essay count; the 2–3/week figure is guidance, not measured.
