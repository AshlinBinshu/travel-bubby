# Indian Economy, Policy and Business Developments of 2026 (Jan–Oct 2026) — IIM Interview Briefing

Research date: 9 October 2026. All facts are dated inline. Where sources conflict or only secondary sources were found, this is flagged. Items marked "(single source)" or "(secondary)" should be re-verified against the primary release before being quoted in an interview.

**The one-paragraph story of 2026 so far:** India entered 2026 on a strong footing (FY26 growth revised to 7.7–7.8%, a new 2022-23 GDP base series, CPI ~2–3% under a new 2024=100 series, repo rate at a cyclical low of 5.25%, GST 2.0 and the Income-tax Act 2025 in place). Then the US–Israel war on Iran (from 28 Feb 2026) and the closure/disruption of the Strait of Hormuz sent Brent to $100–120+, forced a Rs 10/litre excise cut (diesel excise to zero), a Rs 3/litre pump-price hike in May, gold-import curbs and heavy RBI dollar sales. By October: the rupee is at record lows near 96–97/$, forex reserves fell a record $18.3bn in one week to ~$747.6bn, CPI has climbed to 4.82% (Aug) with 5.4% expected for Sept, FPIs have pulled ~Rs 2.5–3 lakh crore out in nine months, Nifty is ~15% below its 5 Jan peak, and on 7 Oct 2026 the RBI delivered its first hike since Feb 2023 (repo to 5.50%, stance to "calibrated tightening") while simultaneously raising FY27 growth to 7.1% — a "strong growth, imported inflation, weak currency" paradox that is the core interview debate. Alongside: BJP won West Bengal, Vijay's TVK won Tamil Nadu, UDF won Kerala; the women's-reservation/delimitation amendment failed in the Lok Sabha; India-UK CETA entered force (15 July); India-EU FTA concluded (27 Jan) with signing due 16 Dec; US-India interim deal still unsigned amid a new US law allowing 100% tariffs on Russian-oil buyers; Jio Platforms' ~Rs 37,700 cr IPO (India's largest ever) is imminent; and a Tata Sons vs Tata Trusts boardroom fight over N. Chandrasekaran's reappointment is live.

## Key Question 1: Macro — growth, inflation, RBI, rupee, fiscal, external, key officials

### Takeaway
FY26 real GDP growth is 7.7% (Provisional Estimate, under the new 2022-23 base) and was revised to 7.8%; Q1 FY27 printed 7.8%. On 7 Oct 2026 the RBI hiked the repo rate 25 bps to 5.50% (first hike since Feb 2023) while raising its FY27 growth forecast to 7.1% and its FY27 CPI forecast to 5.2%, because the Iran-war oil shock, a weak monsoon and a rupee near 96–97/$ have pushed inflation above the 4% target. Key people: RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra; Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman; CEA V. Anantha Nageswaran; SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey.

### Inferences
- The interview "debate" is the growth-inflation-currency trilemma: GDP is tracking 7%+ (RBI, World Bank both at 7.1%), yet the RBI is hiking — because inflation is imported (oil) and supply-driven (monsoon), and because defending the rupee through reserves alone cost a record $18bn in one week. Expect questions on whether rate hikes can address supply-side inflation, and whether "calibrated tightening" is really about the currency.
- The fiscal risk is two-sided: Rs 10/l excise cuts plus a likely higher fertiliser subsidy, versus strong nominal GDP (10.3% in Q1) and GST buoyancy. The 4.3% target is under pressure but not yet breached (41.9% of BE used by August, front-loaded capex).
- India's "4th largest economy" claim is now contestable; the rupee's slide to ~96–97/$ has pushed dollar GDP down. Candidates should know the PPP vs nominal distinction.
- The new base-year series (2022-23) and the new CPI series (2024=100) mean many 2025 figures are not comparable with 2026 figures.

## Key Question 2: Union Budget 2026-27, Income-tax Act 2025, GST reforms and legislation

### Takeaway
Budget 2026-27 (1 Feb 2026, a Sunday) was a fiscal-consolidation-plus-capex budget: capex Rs 12.2 lakh crore, fiscal deficit 4.3% of GDP, no income-tax slab changes, STT hikes and buyback-tax changes for markets. The Income-tax Act 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026. GST 2.0 (two-slab structure from 22 Sep 2025) has not dented collections: September 2026 gross GST was Rs 2.04 lakh crore (+14.7%), the third straight month above Rs 2 lakh crore.
